Each search score is the mean of the release's two runs after it went live, re-scored against today's grades, so older rows can move slightly as grades are added. A dash means the eval was not part of that gate.

What each column measures

Search relevance, development
How well the top 5 search results are ordered, scored by graded relevance (nDCG, top 5) from 0 to 1, on the development queries used for tuning.
Search relevance, held-out
The same score on held-out queries nobody looks at or tunes on: the release gate. Only this average is published.
Found in top 5
Share of find-the-item queries, over both query sets, whose known answer is among the top 5 results.
Small companies
Share of the top 10 results that are about the company asked for, for the smallest listed companies by market value (the bottom three tenths). Only gates that ran it show a value.
Screener
Structured stock screens checked against answers computed independently from the same data, plus requests that must be refused: screens passed of screens run.
Data panel, exact
Share of held-out searches whose data panel (the facts shown beside the results, such as top holders or the next earnings date) shows exactly the kinds of data the question asks for, no more and no less. Only gates that ran it show a value.
Data panel, precision
Share of the kinds of data shown in the data panel, over the held-out searches, that the question asked for.
Verdict
pass: every gate check held. fail: the release was rolled back. accepted on review: a check flagged a drop that review found to be measurement noise, and the release was kept.
